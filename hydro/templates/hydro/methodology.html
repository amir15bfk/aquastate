Reference / Methodology

How the water state is calculated

AquaState reports a current-state score and a separate forward-looking horizon verdict. The MVP is deterministic: every number has a formula, a substitution, a source and a confidence. No AI agent can change the score, the formulas, the inputs or the verdict.

Plain-language answer first. The score tells you how strong the water system is today. The horizon verdict tells you whether the water you have is enough until the next refill. A low score with a secured refill reads “OK until rain”, because the current level is not the binding constraint. The score stays visible and unchanged.

1. The one question

Every dashboard begins with the same question: “Are we covered until the next rain?” The index is a decision aid for a national water analyst, not an official water-accounting standard.

2. Sub-indexes and weights

Each sub-index runs from 0 to 100. Weights sum to 1.00.

Sub-indexWeightWhat it measures
Storage buffer0.36Days of usable stored water at the current net burn, scored against a 90-day target.
Soil moisture0.18Surface soil wetness against the same calendar window in the prior ten years.
Underground reserve0.16Abstraction pressure and water-table direction for mapped aquifers; a labelled root-zone proxy when no aquifer data exists.
Forecast opportunity0.1414-day rain versus the ten-year normal for the same fortnight.
Storage trend0.16Ten-year trajectory, using Sen's slope and a Mann–Kendall significance test.

Storage buffer

Uᵢ = max(0, Sᵢ − Dᵢ)
bᵢ = max(0.05, dᵢ + Eᵢ − qᵢ)
cover_daysᵢ = Uᵢ / bᵢ
B = 100 × Σ dᵢ · min(1, cover_daysᵢ / 90)^0.85 / Σ dᵢ

S is live storage, D dead storage, d allocated demand, q base inflow. Evaporation E = ET0 × area × 0.001 × 0.70, because one millimetre over one square kilometre is 0.001 Mm³. A 90-day runway scores 100; 30 days scores about 39; 60 days about 71.

Soil moisture

p = percentile rank of the current 7-day mean against prior-year windows
a = 50 + 50 × tanh((current − normal) / max(0.05, 0.25 × normal))
M = 0.7p + 0.3a

Underground reserve

abstraction = 100 × clamp((1.25 − extraction/recharge) / 0.75, 0, 1)
trend = 100 × clamp(0.5 + water_table_trend_m_per_year / 3, 0, 1)
G = 0.55 × abstraction + 0.45 × trend

Positive trend means recovery; negative means decline. Fossil systems are flagged as non-renewable on management timescales. If a country has no mapped aquifer, G = 0.6 × root-zone percentile + 0.4 × root-zone trend score and the UI labels it as a proxy.

Forecast opportunity

F = 100 × min(1, (P14 / max(1, Pnormal14)) / 1.25)

A normal fortnight scores 80; 1.25× normal scores 100. Missing forecast data is neutral (50) with low confidence, not treated as drought.

Storage trend

β = Sen's slope in percentage points of capacity per year
T_raw = 100 × clamp(0.5 + β/4, 0, 1)
T = 50 + (T_raw − 50) × (1 − p), using the two-sided Mann–Kendall p-value

A flat series returns β = 0, p = 1, T = 50. The normal approximation is used only with at least eight annual values; ties are corrected. The (1 − p) factor is a deliberate screening shrinkage, not a claim that 1 − p is the probability that a trend is real.

3. Aggregation and bands

base = 0.36B + 0.18M + 0.16G + 0.14F + 0.16T
penalty = 0.15 × max(0, 35 − min(B, M, G, F, T))
WRI = clamp(base − penalty, 0, 100)
BandRangeMeaning
OK≥ 60Current state is resilient.
Watch45 – 59.9One or more pillars need attention.

Critical< 30The current state is the binding constraint.

The bottleneck penalty is deliberately small (maximum 5.25 points). It nudges the score when one pillar is very weak; it cannot manufacture a crisis.

4. Smart Horizon: verdict without rewriting the arithmetic

The horizon simulation runs a 45-day daily balance per reservoir. Forecast rain is used for days 0–15; zero rain is assumed after day 16. Runoff lags rainfall by two days. The screening rainfall response is rain × (capacity / 300) × 0.60, where capacity / 300 has units Mm³ per mm.

rain_gainᵢ(t) = Pᵢ(t − 2) × (Cᵢ / 300) × 0.60
Sᵢ(t+1) = clamp(Sᵢ(t) + qᵢ + rain_gainᵢ(t) − dᵢ − Eᵢ(t), 0, Cᵢ)
relief = first 3-day rain total ≥ 5 × daily demand / national response
relief_day = event day + 1 + 2-day runoff lag

The horizon is secured when the aggregate usable ratio stays above 3% of live capacity through the relief day, every major reservoir stays above 1% of capacity, the reserve at relief is above the emergency threshold, and the refill adds at least two days of national demand and 2% of live capacity. It is buffered when no refill is needed, tight when rain is forecast but the reserve breaches before it arrives, and exposed when no refill is forecast and the runway is short.

Why the score does not jump to 60. The score is the current-state evidence. The horizon verdict is a separate, conditional statement. Rewriting the score would make the arithmetic non-monotone and would hide the current state. The product still says “OK until rain” in the headline and explains the gate; the score stays visible beside it. The horizon is a conditional forecast, not a guarantee.

5. Confidence and honesty

Confidence is the weighted average of source-confidence values. It never changes the score. Live API data is 0.90–0.95; cached data 0.85; modeled reconstruction 0.65–0.75; curated reference 0.68–0.72; synthetic fallback 0.20–0.35. Reservoir storage is modeled or blended from public capacity, demand allocation, NASA POWER rainfall, ET0 and base inflow. It is never presented as SCADA telemetry.

Country switching covers 250 ISO entries. Algeria and Tunisia carry curated screening inventories. Any other country gets a clearly labelled National reference storage pool, plus live satellite soil and forecast data. OpenStreetMap reservoir import is available where that service responds.

6. AI-ready, AI-off

Five agent slots are reserved for future narrative, anomaly, confidence and critique work. They are disabled. The contract rejects any payload that attempts to write core keys; even an enabled slot may only produce an advisory trace, never a mutation of the score, formulas, inputs, pillars or horizon verdict. The audit endpoint records every suggestion, its rationale, confidence, delta, errors and whether it was applied.

7. Limits

  • Reservoir capacities are public screening values; storage is modeled, not operational telemetry.
  • The rainfall-runoff response is a screened coefficient, not a calibrated catchment model.
  • Soil wetness is a point/area reanalysis signal, not a national soil-moisture map.
  • Aquifer figures are public reference estimates, not a numerical groundwater model.
  • The index is a decision aid, not an official water-accounting standard.
